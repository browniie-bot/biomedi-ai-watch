## LinkedIn

This week's BioMed AI Watch is about whether reported performance holds up.

• A meta-research preprint covering 2,001 prediction-model articles in top-quartile journals found AUC, sensitivity and specificity clustering at round numbers. About 1 in 8 ROC curves did not match the reported AUC.
• An audit of a MIMIC-CXR benchmark found that its apparent gains disappeared once patients were kept separate between training and test sets.
• A preprint testing four ECG foundation models on 5.4 million ECGs found performance fell with age. It traces the gap to the data, not the models.
• After moving from one hospital system's data to another's, uncertainty scores did not warn of a mortality model's performance drop (preprint).

On the trial side, a 368-person phase 3 RCT found an AI diabetes-prevention program got people started sooner and kept engagement steadier. Participants still rated human coaching higher.

Most findings above are preprints and not yet peer-reviewed. The common lesson is that patient-level splits, subgroup reporting and post-deployment monitoring belong in the evaluation itself.
